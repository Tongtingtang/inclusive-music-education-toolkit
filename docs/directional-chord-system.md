# Directional Chord System (DCS)

## An Embodied and Spatial Approach to Harmonic Understanding

### Project Status

The Directional Chord System (DCS) is an ongoing framework developed within the broader Kalisarmonia research program.

The system explores how harmonic relationships may be represented through direction, movement, and spatial orientation rather than relying exclusively on conventional notation.

Its primary goal is educational.

The framework seeks to make harmonic relationships more accessible, intuitive, and embodied for diverse learners.

# Why Direction Matters

Traditional harmony often emphasizes chord labels.

Examples include:

* C Major
* G Major
* D Minor
* F Major

While these labels are useful, learners frequently memorize names without fully understanding how chords relate to one another.

The Directional Chord System begins with a different question:

How do harmonic relationships move?

Instead of focusing primarily on chord identity, the framework emphasizes:

* movement
* orientation
* relationship
* destination
* return

Harmony becomes something that is navigated rather than merely named.

---

# Core Principle

The central principle of DCS is that harmonic meaning emerges through directional relationships.

A chord is not understood only as an isolated object.

Its significance depends on:

* where it comes from
* where it moves
* how it relates to a center
* how it contributes to tension and release

The framework therefore prioritizes relationships over labels.

---

# Harmonic Navigation

Within DCS, harmony may be approached as a process of navigation.

Learners are encouraged to think in terms of:

* moving toward a center
* moving away from a center
* expanding
* contracting
* stabilizing
* redirecting

This perspective aligns with embodied experiences of movement through physical space.

---

# The Principle of Center

As in other components of the Kalisarmonia framework, DCS employs the concept of a center.

The center may function as:

* tonic
* reference point
* point of balance
* harmonic home

Musical movement gains meaning through its relationship to this center.

Without orientation, direction cannot be perceived.

---

# Direction Rather Than Labels

Traditional notation often answers the question:

"What chord is this?"

DCS additionally asks:

"Where is this harmony moving?"

This shift encourages learners to focus on process rather than classification.

The framework therefore investigates directional representations such as:

* approach
* departure
* return
* convergence
* divergence
* continuation

These concepts may be represented visually, spatially, or pedagogically.

---

# Arrow-Based Representation

One practical feature of the system is the use of directional symbols and arrows.

These visual representations may help learners identify:

* movement tendencies
* harmonic flow
* structural relationships
* patterns of resolution

The purpose of these symbols is not to replace conventional notation.

Instead, they function as an additional layer of understanding.

---

# Cross-Instrument Application

DCS is designed to operate independently of specific instruments.

The framework may be applied within:

* Kolintang
* Angklung
* keyboard instruments
* guitar
* vocal ensembles
* classroom activities

Because the system emphasizes relationships rather than instrument-specific techniques, it may support diverse learning environments.

---

# Relationship to Jacob's Hand

Jacob's Hand explores relational and spatial logic.

DCS applies similar principles to harmonic movement.

If Jacob's Hand investigates how relationships may be organized through embodied structures, DCS investigates how those relationships behave within harmonic processes.

The two frameworks are complementary but address different questions.

---

# Relationship to Kolintang Hand

Kolintang Hand focuses primarily on participation and ensemble interaction.

DCS focuses primarily on harmonic navigation.

When used together, the two frameworks may support both musical coordination and harmonic understanding.
